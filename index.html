Core Skills & Technique 🎸

The building blocks of great music production. Master these and everything else becomes much easier — and much more fun.

🎵

Music Theory for Producers

You don't need to be a theory nerd — just know enough to create

Chords & Harmony

  • Major & minor chords — the foundation of all music
  • Common chord progressions: I–V–vi–IV, ii–V–I
  • 7th chords — add colour and emotion
  • Transposing to different keys

Melody & Rhythm

  • Scales: major, minor, pentatonic — great for melodies
  • Rhythm: time signatures, note values, groove
  • Song structure: intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro
  • Tension & release — the secret of memorable hooks
💡 Pro tip: Start a "chord journal" — every time you find a chord combo you love in a song, write it down. Over time you'll have a personal toolkit of go-to progressions.
🎤

Recording Technique

Your studio has amazing gear — here's how to use it well

🎹
Clavinova / MIDI Piano
Connect via MIDI or USB. Set Logic's track to "Software Instrument" and choose a piano sound. Record-enable the track, hit record, and play!
🎸
Guitar Recording
Use an audio interface (or the studio's DI box). Plug in, set input level so the meter hits around –12dB to –6dB. Logic has great built-in guitar amp sims — try Amp Designer!
🥁
V-Drums / Drum Machine
V-Drums send MIDI — connect to Logic and use your own drum kit sound or Drummer plugin. Great for realistic-feeling rhythms and live drum performance.

🎚️ The Golden Rules of Recording

📊 Gain staging: levels should peak at –12 to –6dB, never clip (red)
🔇 Quiet room: close windows, turn off fans before recording
🎧 Monitor with headphones: Sony MDR-7506 are professional-grade, trust them
💾 Save often: Cmd+S every few minutes, and use Logic's Auto-Save
🎛️

Effects & Processing

The tools that transform a raw recording into something polished

EQ Equalisation

EQ lets you boost or cut specific frequencies. Think of it as a "tone control" on steroids. Use Channel EQ in Logic. High-pass filter on vocals to remove rumble. Boost highs on a guitar to add presence. Cut muddy frequencies (around 200–400Hz) when things sound boomy.

COMP Compression

Compression evens out the volume of a sound — loud parts get quieter, so everything sits at a consistent level. Logic's Compressor plugin is excellent. Start with the "Platinum Analog Tape" preset on vocals. Compression makes drums punchier and vocals more "upfront" in a mix.

REVERB Reverb & Delay

Reverb makes a sound feel like it's in a room or hall — adds depth and space. Logic's ChromaVerb looks and sounds amazing. Delay creates echoes — Tape Delay in Logic sounds warm and musical. Rule of thumb: use reverb subtly; too much makes a mix sound like a bathroom recording!

AUTO Automation

Automation lets you record changes over time — like a volume fade, a filter sweep, or a pan that moves from left to right. Press A in Logic to see automation lanes. Try automating the volume of your chorus to be 2dB louder than the verse — it makes a huge difference!

🎚️

Mixing & Mastering Basics

Make your tracks sound clean, balanced, and ready to share

Mixing

  • Balance volume levels — everything should be audible
  • Pan instruments left/right for width (bass usually stays centre)
  • EQ to give each sound its own frequency "space"
  • Add reverb/delay to create depth and dimension
  • Reference your mix on headphones AND speakers

Mastering

  • Mastering is the final step — it makes your track "release ready"
  • Use Logic's Master Bus (Stereo Out channel)
  • Add a limiter (Adaptive Limiter) to prevent clipping
  • Target –14 LUFS for streaming (Spotify, Apple Music)
  • Compare your master to a professional track you love

Audio Engineering Areas 🎚️

Audio production has so many exciting paths. Explore each one — you might be surprised which one sparks something in you. Many professionals work across several areas.

🏢
Studio Production & Recording
Working in a recording studio to capture and produce music. Involves mic placement, session management, working with artists, mixing, and mastering. This is the "classic" music producer/engineer path.
Mixing Mastering Session Work
🔊
Live Sound Engineering
Running sound at concerts, theatres, and events. Uses mixing desks, PA systems, stage monitors, and in-ear monitors. Fast-paced, social, and every show is different. Great for people who love live music AND tech.
FOH Engineer Monitor Mixing PA Systems
🎮
Game Audio
Creating sound effects, music, and interactive soundscapes for video games. Uses tools like Wwise and FMOD alongside your DAW. Games need sounds that react to player actions — a unique creative challenge! One of the fastest-growing areas.
Sound Design Wwise / FMOD Interactive Music
⭐ Hot career right now — game audio is booming and needs talented producers with your skillset!
🎬
TV, Film & Broadcast
Composing and producing music for film, TV shows, trailers, documentaries, and radio. Includes sync licensing (getting your music placed in media), library music production, and working to picture — matching music to on-screen action.
Sync Licensing Score to Picture Library Music
📺
Advertising & Commercial Music
Writing short-form music for adverts, brand campaigns, and commercials. Working to tight briefs, fast turnarounds, and specific emotional or brand tones. Well-paid when you establish yourself — a successful ad jingle can earn significant royalties.
Jingles Brand Music Brief Writing
🔊
Other Exciting Pathways
The audio world has so many niches!
🎙️ Podcast Production — a booming industry with great freelance potential
🔔 Sonic Branding — creating sounds for brands (Intel chime, Apple startup)
📱 App & UI Sound Design — the sounds phones and apps make
🎭 Theatre Sound Design — sound for stage productions
🎓 Music Technology Teaching — helping others learn what you know

🗺️ Which Path is Right for You?

The honest answer? You don't need to choose yet — and many professionals do several of these simultaneously. The best approach is to try them all through your challenges and projects. Make a game sound effect, write a 30-second jingle, score a short video clip. See what excites you most, then follow that thread.

Business & Career 💡

Knowing the business side of music early gives you a massive advantage. Most talented artists struggle because they don't understand this stuff — you will.

🌍 The Reality of the Music Industry

The music industry is exciting, rewarding, and genuinely pays well for skilled professionals — but it's not like a standard job. Here's what to expect:

Challenges to know about

  • 📊 Income can be variable, especially early on
  • 🤝 Networking is genuinely important — who you know matters
  • 💻 Most work is freelance — you'll be self-employed
  • 🔄 The industry changes fast — keep learning constantly

Reasons to be excited

  • 🎮 Game audio, TV, and advertising all pay extremely well
  • 🌐 You can work from anywhere — including your home studio
  • 🎓 Skills transfer across many industries (film, tech, media)
  • ✨ Every day is different and genuinely creative

© Copyright, PRS & Licensing

Copyright

When you write and record an original piece of music, you automatically own the copyright. This means nobody can use your music without your permission. In the UK, copyright lasts for 70 years after the composer's death.

PRS for Music & MCPS

PRS collects royalties when your music is performed or broadcast publicly (TV, radio, live venues). MCPS collects mechanical royalties when your music is reproduced (CDs, streaming, downloads). You should register with PRS as soon as you release music.

Sync Licensing

Synchronisation licensing is when your music is used in film, TV, games, or adverts. This is one of the best income sources for composers — one well-placed sync can earn thousands of pounds.

Metadata

Always embed metadata in your audio files — your name, composition title, year, ISRC code, and contact details. This ensures you get paid even if your music is shared without attribution. Logic Pro can embed this when you export.

🛤️ Career Routes to Explore

🎓

Music Technology, Music Production, or Audio Engineering degrees at unis like BIMM, LIPA, Leeds, Coventry. BTECs at local colleges are also very solid and industry-respected.

🔧
Apprenticeships

Creative & Cultural Skills run audio/production apprenticeships. You earn while you learn, get industry experience, and come out with a qualification. Highly recommended.

💻
Self-Taught / Portfolio Route

Many successful producers are entirely self-taught. If your portfolio is strong enough, it often matters more than a degree — especially in game audio and online music.

🤝
Networking & Work Experience

Contact local studios, radio stations, game companies, and theatres. Ask for work experience or shadowing. Most industry jobs come from who you know — start building relationships now.

🖥️ Your Studio Setup — Explained

You have access to some seriously professional gear. Here's what each piece does:

💻 MacBook Neo
Your main production machine. Apple Silicon means Logic Pro runs blazingly fast — you can run dozens of plugins without breaking a sweat.
🎹 Akai Keyboard/Pads
MIDI controller for playing melodies, chords, and triggering drum sounds. The pads are great for beat-making — each pad can trigger a different drum sound.
🎧 Sony MDR-7506
Industry-standard studio headphones used in professional studios worldwide. They're flat and accurate — what you hear is genuinely what's there. Always mix on these before finalising.
🎹 Yamaha Clavinova
High-quality digital piano with weighted keys — excellent for recording realistic piano performances via MIDI. Use it to play in melodies and chord parts with real expression.
🥁 Roland V-Drums
Electronic drum kit that sends MIDI — record real drum performances with actual sticks. Much more organic-sounding than clicking in notes. Try layering V-Drum performances with Logic's Drummer plugin.
🎸 Guitars
Record direct into your audio interface (DI) and use Logic's Amp Designer for virtual amp tones, or mic the amp with a Shure SM57 if you have one. Both acoustic and electric work brilliantly in Logic.

🛡️ Hearing Health — Really Important!

Your hearing is your most valuable tool as a music producer. Hearing damage is permanent and cumulative — it's essential to protect your ears from the start of your career.

🔊 Keep headphone volume below 85dB — if someone else can hear from your headphones, it's too loud
⏰ Take a 10-minute break every hour of studio work — your ears (and brain) need rest
🎧 Use the volume limiter in your MacBook & iPhone settings — set to 85dB maximum
🎶 At live gigs and concerts, wear ear plugs — musician's earplugs (like Earpeace) preserve sound quality

Parent & Mentor Notes 👨‍👧

A guide for supporting a young aspiring music producer — how to help, when to step back, and how to make the most of the studio together.

💜 How to Be the Best Supporter

🎯
Let her lead the sessions

When in the studio together, resist the urge to take over — even if you could do something faster. Ask "what do you want to try next?" rather than directing. Her creative confidence will grow enormously when she drives the session.

🎧
Ask to hear her work

Regularly asking "can I listen to what you've been making?" shows you value her work. Listen all the way through, then ask what she's most proud of in the track. Specific, genuine praise ("I love that bass line at 0:45") means more than general compliments.

🤔
Ask curious questions, not evaluative ones

"What were you going for with this?" is better than "Is that supposed to sound like that?" Curiosity invites conversation; judgement shuts it down. The teenage brain is especially sensitive to perceived criticism of their creative work.

🔇
Protect her studio time

Deep creative work requires uninterrupted time. If she's in the flow, avoid calling her for non-urgent things. Even 90 minutes of uninterrupted studio time a few times a week will lead to remarkable progress over months.

🔍 How to Review Her Work in the Studio

When she wants to share or you're reviewing together, here's a useful framework:

Great things to say

  • "What's your favourite part of this track?"
  • "How did you make that sound?"
  • "This is really coming along — I can hear how much you've improved."
  • "What do you want to work on next?"
  • "I really like the [specific element]."

Things to avoid

  • Unsolicited technical criticism
  • Comparing to professional tracks ("but it doesn't sound like…")
  • Grabbing the keyboard or mouse without asking
  • Checking your phone while listening
  • "Why don't you try…" (unless asked)

🌟 The Bigger Picture

Music production is a brilliant skill regardless of whether it becomes a career. It develops: creativity, attention to detail, patience, technical problem-solving, listening skills, and emotional intelligence. Even if her path changes, these skills transfer everywhere.

The fact that she has a full studio at home and a supportive parent willing to invest in her passion is genuinely rare and special. The most important thing is that she enjoys the journey — the skills and opportunities will follow naturally from that joy.

"The goal isn't to become a professional musician. The goal is to develop a deep love for the craft, the skills to realise your creative vision, and the confidence to keep creating no matter what."
